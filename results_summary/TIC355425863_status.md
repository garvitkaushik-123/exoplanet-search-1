## Concerns (why this is not a candidate yet)
1. **Grazing, V-shaped fit.** A joint batman fit gives Rp/R★ = 0.125 (about 10.8 R⊕), b = 0.92 and T14 = 2.98 h. Grazing geometry is the classic eclipsing-binary false-positive configuration.
2. **The duration is too short for every surviving alias.** At b = 0.92 the expected T14 is 3.6–8.8 h for P = 46.65–699.8 d, while 2.98 h is observed. That needs an even higher b, an eccentric orbit, or a different host.
3. **The depths differ by about 25% between the events** in every pipeline. This could come from dilution or background subtraction (sector 95 background is about 60 times sector 69's), or the two events could be the primary and secondary eclipses of an eccentric EB.
4. **Neighbours: RULED OUT by difference imaging (2026-11-05).** TESScut FFI difference images (`diff_image.py`, plots `diffimg_355425863_s69.png` and `_s95.png`) put the lost light on the target. The difference-centroid offset is 0.18 px (≈4″) in sector 69 and 0.08 px (≈2″) in sector 95. Random-time noise is 0.43 and 0.38 px, and difference-image S/N is 22 and 29. The nearest capable neighbour (TIC 389758822) is 4.2 px from the difference centroid. What remains is a source within about 4″: a bound companion, or the target itself as a grazing eclipsing binary.
